# The Weather in the Ink

**Landscape read as atmosphere and mood rather than geography — the sketchbook's own "vellum chinese ink painting landscape," extended through the named influence Kawase Hasui and the Muromachi ink painter Josetsu.**

## Summary
Two named touchstones frame this. [[Artist - kawase hasui|Kawase Hasui]] is listed on the
[[influences|influences page]] (as "kawase_husui" — the site's own long-standing spelling)
and is by far the archive's most repeated landscape printmaker: rain at Gokokuji, evening
at Itako, snow at a dozen shrines — the place is almost incidental next to the weather
falling on it. Josetsu's [[Hy nen zu by Josetsu (c. 1413) 0834942f|Hyōnenzu]] (c. 1413) is
two centuries older than ukiyo-e and belongs to the same lineage of ink landscape as
atmosphere over inventory. The [[ideas sketchbook 2020 25|sketchbook]]'s own shorthand for
a drawing project — "vellum chinese ink painting landscape" — sits in that same line:
landscape as a study of held atmosphere and empty space, not a record of place.

## Key points
- Hasui's prints repeat because the subject really is the weather (rain, snow, dusk, moon)
  recurring across dozens of different towns — not the towns themselves.
- Josetsu predates the ukiyo-e tradition by two centuries; the taste for atmospheric
  landscape reaches back to Muromachi ink painting, not just Meiji/shin-hanga printmaking.
- Distinct from [[Spontaneity and Elegance]] (about the *gesture* of the ink line) and from
  [[The Spatial Web]] (digital, diagrammatic space) — this is the third, natural-atmospheric
  kind of held space.

## Connections
- [[Spontaneity and Elegance]] — the shared ink tradition, aimed at place instead of gesture.
- [[The Spatial Web]] — the synthetic, diagrammatic counterpart to natural atmospheric space.
- [[Art Fills the God-Shaped Hole]] — landscape as a secular sublime, adjacent to the sacred images collected there.
- [[Artist - kawase hasui]] — the shin-hanga touchstone.
