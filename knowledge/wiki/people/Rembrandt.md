# Rembrandt

**Dutch painter (1606–1669); named in both the diary and the sketchbook not for the finished paintings but for the sketches — prized as unfiltered access to the artist's mind at work.**

## Summary
Diary, 2024-09-25: *"i like some of rembrandt's sketches more than his actual
paintings. same reason why i love piano pieces by composers more than actual
orchestral arrangements. just pure unfiltered captures of the artist's mind at
work."* The [[ideas sketchbook 2020 25|sketchbook]] separately notes "rembrandt's
diana" — a specific work kept as a working reference alongside other artists'
sketches (Degas' dancers appears two lines later in the same list).

## Key points
- The comparison isn't really about Rembrandt specifically — it's a stated
  aesthetic preference (sketch over finished work, solo piece over full
  orchestration) that Rembrandt happens to be the anchoring example for.
- Matches the vault's existing [[Spontaneity and Elegance]] concept almost
  exactly: the sketch as the "felt mark," prized above the more laboured,
  fully-resolved version of the same idea.
