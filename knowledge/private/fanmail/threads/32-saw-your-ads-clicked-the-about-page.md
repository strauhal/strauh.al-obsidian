## 2025-08-19 - Correspondent 29

**Direction:** incoming

Wild how influential /lit/ was (and continues to be) for like... what, 5k people max in the US? Whenever I think about /lit/ or /mu/ I think about that Velvet Underground quip that's like "6 people heard that record but every single one started a band" or something. Occasionally I'll see someone's shelf or hear someone online talk about books and I'll realize that they were also a /lit/ poster. Was very influential for me personally from 2012-15 or so. Will never forget opening a thread for the first time where someone asked "what's the final boss of /lit/" and someone posted a jpg of Finnegans Wake and I still remember thinking "who the hell is james joyce?" lmao

Thx for the diary link. Reminds me of Meg Boyle's thing from the alt-lit scene way back when. Agreed re; liking Rembrandt's sketches more than his paintings -- I don't know a ton about art, but I'm always way more drawn to an artist's notebooks, sketchbooks, drafts etc than I am their finished pieces. Something about how the notebook is raw creative force made tangible and the finished piece is a posture -- which isn't necessarily bad of course, but it's a different kind of energy. I remember seeing a .jpg of Picasso's poetry notebooks where he just daubed paint mid-sentence instead of finding the right color word. That kinda stuff you know? Happy to hear about a sequence of most wonderful events that reignited your will to live.

Re; MFA and art; would love any recommendations you have for contemporary/modern shit that you enjoy, especially artists who you think are doing something new, interesting, exciting. Always trying to expand my taste.

Disintegration Loops is beautiful. I used to throw it on and take really long walks around town when I was in college. One of my all time favs.

Love your website btw. Drawn to the repository aspect, kinda like a 21st c. dymaxion chronofile thing. I think I'm going to steal your idea and make my own website. How did you do yours? I know literally nothing about web design other than "I've seen a preroll ad for Squarespace." Been kicking around a few folders on my laptop/pgs in some notebooks that I've been meaning to actually do something with.

## 2025-08-23 - Correspondent 29

**Direction:** incoming

Oh these are awesome. Never heard of Beuys before but that photo of him in the felt cloak during I Like America & America Likes Me is striking. That sculpture The End of the 20th Century reminds me of the Yeats poem The Nineteenth Century and After. Wikipedia says he never really grappled with his Nazi past; I can buy that he didn't feel he had another option and couldn't desert/flee for whatever reason, but surprising he didn't make any art investigating it. What's the artworld take on this? Did he make art about it, directly/indirectly?

Oh /lit/ and Jest. I also remember that era on /lit/. (Funny you mention it, I'm in a IJ reading group right now with a few friends. Second go-round. Both funnier and more annoying than I remember it being.) It was IJ, Harold Bloom, and Joyce for a long, long while. During Trump I everyone was posting Guenon. Mishima's having a moment. 4chan/imageboards tend to be 1 to 2 years ahead of the right-wing zeitgeist, so I spend more time than I should trying to keep up with them. Right now you can feel the ghost of Carl Schmitt behind a lot of posts, so I expect Vance to mention the friend/enemy distinction sometime in '26 or '27.

LOVE the first smartboard drawing from the students, the circles/arcs in the lower right especially. Always interesting to see how kids (I'm assuming they're young kids) respond to art before someone tells them anything other than photorealism is bad.

Thanks for the influences/art recommendations -- tell your friend I think his work's great. Schiele is killer. My fav from your list is def Rosman. I don't have you but you've still got my heart is eerily prescient. Can't recall the actual wording offhand, but there's this great Freud quote where he says something like "whenever I think I've had an original thought, I find that poets always got there first." Think it applies to art in all forms. (Speaking of poets and art, you might enjoy Frank O'Hara: Poet Among Painters by Perloff -- O'Hara was a curator at MoMA for most of his life, and the book draws on O'Hara's poems, diaries, and letters to show how contemporary visual arts influenced his work. Great stuff. Speaking of poets and art in a more extended, looser sense, you a Death Grips fan? What do you think about MC Ride's paintings?)

Good to know about wysiwyg editors and thanks for the ok re; theft. Be on the lookout.
